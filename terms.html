4. Wholesaling and Contract Assignment

Important. Transition Properties may operate as a real estate wholesaler. This means we may:
  1. Enter a purchase and sale agreement with you to acquire your property
  2. Hold an equitable interest in your property via that contract
  3. Assign our contractual interest to a third-party end buyer prior to closing
  4. Receive an assignment fee or profit from that transfer
  5. Have the end buyer (rather than us) close on the purchase

The price we agree to pay you may differ from the price the end buyer pays us for the assignment. By entering a purchase agreement with us, you acknowledge and consent to this practice.

This is legal in Florida and is a recognized real estate practice. You retain the right to:

  • Have an independent attorney review any contract before signing
  • Negotiate the terms of any agreement
  • Decline to sign any contract for any reason
  • Cancel or rescind contracts as permitted by law

5. No Guaranteed Outcomes

We do not guarantee that we will make an offer, that an offer will match your asking price or any market valuation, that a transaction will close successfully, a specific timeline for closing, or any particular price, terms, or outcome.

Our offers are based on our independent analysis of the property, market conditions, and our investment criteria. Offers are non-binding until a written purchase and sale agreement is signed by both parties.

6. Florida Foreclosure Disclosure

If your property is in foreclosure or you are facing financial hardship, please understand:

  • We are NOT a foreclosure rescue service
  • We do NOT offer loan modifications, forbearance agreements, or "save your home" services
  • We do NOT charge upfront fees for any services
  • We may offer to purchase your property at a price we determine — that is the only service we provide

You have rights under Florida law regarding foreclosure-related transactions, including potential rights of rescission under Florida Statute § 501.1377 (Foreclosure Rescue Fraud Prevention Act). We strongly encourage you to consult a Florida attorney before signing any document affecting your property if you are in foreclosure or pre-foreclosure.

Free resources:
