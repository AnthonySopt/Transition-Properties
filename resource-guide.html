Section 1

If You Are Facing Foreclosure — Start Here

The earlier you act, the more options you have. Do not wait for a court date. Do not ignore mail from your lender or the clerk of court. Florida is a judicial foreclosure state, which means your lender must sue you in court to take your home — and that process creates opportunities to defend yourself, negotiate, or qualify for help.

First steps (take these today)

  1. Open every piece of mail from your lender and from the county clerk. A foreclosure complaint gives you only 20 days to file a written answer before a default judgment can be entered.
  2. Call your mortgage servicer — the company you send payments to — and ask for the Loss Mitigation department. Ask about forbearance, repayment plans, loan modification, short sale, or deed-in-lieu.
  3. Call a free HUD-approved housing counselor at 1-800-569-4287 or Lee County Housing Development Corporation at (239) 652-1966. This help is always free.
  4. If a lawsuit has been filed against you, contact Florida Rural Legal Services or your county legal aid office for free foreclosure defense representation.
  5. Gather pay stubs, tax returns, hardship letter, mortgage statement, and all foreclosure notices — so you are ready when help arrives.
Warning — do not do these things:
  • Do not pay anyone who asks for an upfront fee to stop your foreclosure or modify your loan. Under Florida law, that alone is a red flag.
  • Do not sign your deed over to anyone promising to make your mortgage payments for you. This is the most common equity-stripping scam.
  • Do not stop talking to your lender. Avoidance is what turns a cure-able delinquency into a foreclosure judgment.
  • Do not abandon the house before you are legally required to. You may still owe the deficiency and you lose negotiating leverage the day you leave.

Free HUD-Approved Housing Counseling

HUD-approved housing counselors are federally certified, free, and legally prohibited from taking advance fees. They will review your full financial picture, help you talk to your servicer, prepare you for loss mitigation applications, and evaluate whether any state or federal programs can help you.

Section 4

Mortgage & Housing Assistance Programs

Florida Homeowner Assistance Fund (HAF)

The Florida HAF program was funded by the federal American Rescue Plan Act. HAF could provide up to $50,000 per eligible household for past-due mortgage, property tax, insurance, HOA, and utility payments, with priority for homeowners impacted by COVID-19.

Current status: As of the latest tracking, Florida's HAF program is listed as closed to new applications. Federal HAF funds are scheduled to sunset September 30, 2026. Programs occasionally reopen if funds are returned or reallocated — worth checking before assuming the door is closed.
Florida HAF Customer Assistance Center
1-833-987-8997 CFPB: HAF program info

Lee County Rent / Mortgage & Water Assistance

Lee County Human & Veteran Services
(239) 533-7900 HVSHousing@leegov.com leegov.com/dhs/assistance

For residents with past-due rent, mortgage, move-in costs, or water bills caused by a temporary loss of income. The application portal opens at noon Mon–Fri and closes once the daily limit is reached. Must be Lee County resident; income at or below 60% of Florida State Median Income.

Other mortgage relief paths to ask your lender about

  • Forbearance: a temporary pause or reduction of payments, with a repayment plan after.
  • Loan modification: a permanent change to your loan terms (rate, length, principal).
  • Partial claim (FHA loans): HUD pays a lump sum to bring you current; you repay it when you sell or refinance.
  • Repayment plan: you add a portion of the past-due amount to your regular payments over a set period.
  • Short sale: the lender agrees to accept less than the balance owed if you sell the home.
  • Deed-in-lieu of foreclosure: you voluntarily sign over the deed, and the lender releases you from the debt.
  • Fannie Mae RefiNow / Freddie Mac Refi Possible: refinancing options for lower-income borrowers with limited equity.

Section 10

Protect Yourself from Foreclosure Rescue Scams

Foreclosure lists are public records. That means anyone behind on payments will quickly attract phone calls, door-knockers, postcards, and text messages promising to "save" the home. Some of these offers are legitimate. Many are scams designed to strip your equity, steal your deed, or take fees for work that is never performed.

Red flags — walk away if you hear any of these:
  • They demand an upfront fee before providing any service.
  • They ask you to sign over your deed, even temporarily, with a promise to "rent it back" or "buy it back later."
  • They tell you to stop paying your mortgage and send payments to them instead.
  • They guarantee they can stop your foreclosure or modify your loan — no one can guarantee that.
  • They tell you not to contact your lender, a lawyer, or a HUD counselor.
  • They pressure you to sign documents you haven't read.
  • They claim to be "government-affiliated" or use HUD/FHA/VA logos without authorization.

Common scam structures to recognize

  • Phantom help: an upfront fee for "loan modification" or "foreclosure consultation," after which the scammer disappears.
  • Bailout / equity-stripping: you transfer the deed in exchange for a promised lease and buyback option, but the terms are structured so you can never repurchase.
  • Bait-and-switch: documents presented as a forbearance or assistance agreement are actually a transfer of title or a power of attorney.
  • Forensic loan audit: a high fee for an "audit" of your loan documents that supposedly forces your lender to modify — it rarely works.

Before you sign anything

  1. Read every page. Never sign a blank document. Never sign under pressure.
  2. Ask for a copy of any offer in writing and take 24–48 hours to review it.
  3. Show the offer to a HUD-approved housing counselor or legal aid attorney. Both are free.
  4. Verify the company with the Florida Attorney General and the Florida Department of Business & Professional Regulation.

Report suspected scams

What a legitimate cash offer looks like: Working with a cash buyer or real estate investor is not a scam by itself — many homeowners legitimately sell their home to avoid foreclosure when other options don't fit. The difference is transparency. A legitimate buyer will explain the numbers, give you time to review, recommend you talk to your lender and a counselor, and never ask you to sign over your deed without a clean closing through a licensed title company.
Section 11

A Final Word

A foreclosure notice is not the end of the road. It is a starting line — a point at which you are forced to make decisions you would rather not make, with help available that you may not have known existed. The agencies and programs in this guide exist specifically to give you options. Use them.

Call before you go. Bring documentation. Ask questions. If the first number doesn't work, try the next one. If one program says no, another may say yes. And if you are overwhelmed, start with one phone call — 2-1-1 — and let them help you build the list.

You are not alone in this. Help is free. The earlier you act, the more you can save.
